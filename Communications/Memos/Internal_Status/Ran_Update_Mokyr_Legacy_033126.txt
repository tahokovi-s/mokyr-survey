On Mokyr, I have a first pass of the generation-based descriptives and a working interactive network visualization. A meaningful share of the time before spring break went into building and repeatedly adjusting the Python processing pipeline that takes the raw survey data and converts it into a format I can use for visualization and analysis. At this stage, the descriptives I am computing directly from the survey fields are:

- Head counts by generation: Joel's direct students, their students, later descendants, and network totals.
- Subtree sizes: how many known descendants sit under each of Joel's direct students.
- Student supervision: who reports having supervised PhD students and where Q12 says "yes" but Q12a is missing.
- PhD-year distributions: cohort ranges, means, medians, and cohort-specific supervision rates.
- PhD institutions: counts by doctoral institution for Joel's direct students.
- Current employers: counts by institution or employer.
- Geography: country and U.S. state distributions where available.
- Role/status: faculty, graduate student, industry, government, policy, etc.

I will finish these survey-field stats first. That gives us a descriptive baseline grounded in data we collected ourselves.

I am also attaching the current network HTML (mokyr-genealogy-033126.html). It should be opened on a computer or laptop rather than on a phone, since the layout and controls are designed for a larger screen. To launch it, you should just download the attachment and open the HTML file in a browser (Chrome, Safari, or Firefox all work; no special software is needed). The search box lets you jump to a specific person, institution, or employer; "Show non-respondent nodes" adds the non-respondent names currently carried in the working graph from Q12a and curated manual additions; "Show name labels" toggles the labels on and off; and "Show medium-confidence links" toggles the advisory links that are still being carried as provisional rather than fully confirmed. You can also hover over any node for details, drag nodes around to untangle clusters, and zoom/pan freely.
